  problem:
    text: |
      Global Virtuoso builds and runs specialist operating teams for critical
      business functions. It has operated since 2007 and works from two
      offices, in Manila and Dubai.

      By 2026 the brand had grown faster than the system meant to hold it
      together. The same logo existed in colors that did not match each other.
      Files were named in ways nobody could follow, so the current version was
      never obvious. The brand's language no longer matched how the company
      described itself, and the previous manual said little about the materials
      the team produced every day: emails, letters, job ads, social posts,
      signage.

      The company needed one manual that settled every question once, a 2026
      logo set that could stand next to the original 2018 marks, and templates
      the team could reuse without asking a designer each time.

  process:
    text: |
      I started with structure: six parts and twenty-one sections, from
      introduction through governance and reference. Settling the skeleton
      first meant every later decision had a place to live.

      Then I prepared the pages in order, building the system from the ground
      up: logo, color, typography, icons, and the applications that sit on top
      of them.

      The CEO's corrections arrived in rounds. I treated each round as a
      checklist, applied every change exactly as written, and verified it
      against the list before moving on. Before release, I read the finished
      manual page by page against the final list, checking every rule it sets,
      down to spacing, color values, and the way dates and file names are
      written.
    media: []
  decisions:
    text: |
      **Color.** Dark Blue #0076B6 and Light Blue #0095C0 carry the identity.
      Yellow #F7CE46 stays a restrained accent with a closed list of three
      uses, so it never becomes a background. Deep Navy #040F24 is the dark
      ground.

      **Type.** I chose DM Sans for all brand and marketing use. It is free through
      Google Fonts and licensed for commercial use, so the whole team can use
      it without a licensing barrier. Times New Roman is reserved for formal
      correspondence.

      **Icons.** One style everywhere: a 24 by 24 grid, 2 px stroke, rounded caps
      and joins, one color per icon. Icons clarify, they do not decorate. New
      icons come from the same library, so the set stays consistent as it
      grows.

      **Logo.** I rebuilt the 2026 logo set as one consistent system: a horizontal
      primary, a vertical lockup, and the standalone symbol, each in full
      color, monotone, and white reverse, plus square masters sized for
      favicons, profile images, and app icons. Every file sits on a transparent
      background, trimmed to the mark and named the way the manual names it, so
      the right version is never in doubt.

      **Templates as systems, not images.** The letterheads and envelopes are
      real, structured Word templates: a first page, a continuation header,
      footers, and paragraph styles, set up for both the Manila and Dubai
      offices with each office's address and page formats. For the business
      cards I turned what had been a slow, one-off design task into a fast,
      repeatable production process, so a new card for any employee comes out
      quickly and consistently, with its contact QR code and print-ready files
      matched to each office's printer. The team can produce cards at the speed
      hiring demands, without the brand drifting.
